# Shopify B2B in 2026: Plus vs Basic, What Catalogs Actually Do, and Which Apps You Really Need

*Last updated: August 2026*

If you searched for this topic six months ago, almost every article told you the same thing: **"To sell wholesale on Shopify, you need Shopify Plus — or you need to buy an app."**

That advice is now out of date, and following it will cost you money.

In April 2026, Shopify opened its native B2B feature set to **every paid plan**. Company profiles, wholesale pricing, net payment terms, minimum order quantities — all of it now ships with Basic. Most of the "top 10 wholesale apps" listicles ranking on Google were written before that change and have not been revised.

This guide is written after the change. It covers what you actually get, where the real walls still are, how to test everything safely before launch, and — the part most articles get wrong — which apps you genuinely need versus which ones duplicate something Shopify already gives you for free.

## 1. What changed in April 2026

Shopify B2B used to be a Plus-tier privilege. As of April 2026, it is available on **Basic, Grow, Advanced and Plus**.

This is a bigger deal than it sounds. A merchant on the cheapest paid plan can now do things that used to require a $2,500/month contract:

- Create company accounts with multiple contacts and multiple locations
- Show a completely different price to a logged-in wholesale buyer than to a retail visitor, on the same product page
- Offer Net 30 terms and let the order ship before payment clears
- Enforce minimum order quantities and carton-based increments
- Let buyers reorder past orders in one click

Plus did not become worthless — but its role changed. It is no longer the gate to B2B. It is now the answer to **scale** and **payment flexibility**, which we will get to in section 4.

## 3. What a catalog actually is — and why it matters

If you take one concept away from this article, make it this one. Catalogs are the engine of Shopify B2B, and almost every limitation you will hit traces back to them.

A **catalog** bundles two decisions:

1. **Which products this buyer can see** (product availability)
2. **What those products cost them** (the price list)

Inside that, two more controls live at the variant level:

- **Volume pricing** — 10 units at $9, 50 units at $8, 100 units at $7
- **Quantity rules** — minimum 12, increments of 12, i.e. you sell by the carton, not the piece

![How a B2B catalog works](images/02-how-catalogs-work.svg)

### A concrete example

You sell a cotton t-shirt. Retail price is $50.

- **Catalog A "Gold Distributor"** → all products, $30, minimum 24 pieces
- **Catalog B "Silver Dealer"** → all products, $37, minimum 12 pieces
- **Catalog C "Export Buyer"** → only 5 SKUs, priced in euros

A Gold buyer logs in and sees $30. A Silver buyer sees $37. A walk-in retail visitor sees $50. **Same store, same product page, same URL.** That is the whole magic of B2B on Shopify, and it works on Basic.

### The wall you will eventually hit

On Basic, Grow and Advanced, you get **three active catalogs in total**, and they can only be assigned to **B2B markets** — never directly to an individual company.

Here is what that means in practice. Say you have forty wholesale accounts. One of them, Rahman Traders, has been with you ten years, and you have agreed to give them an extra 18% off because of that relationship.

**On Basic, you cannot do this.** There is no mechanism to attach a price list to one company. Rahman Traders has to go into the Gold bucket, and everyone in the Gold bucket pays the same.

On Plus, you would create a catalog called "Rahman Traders — Negotiated" and assign it to that company alone. Done in two minutes.

> **Prerequisite worth knowing:** to use B2B catalogs on Basic, Grow or Advanced, your store must be on [new Shopify Markets](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets).

## 4. Basic vs Plus: the five real differences

Strip away the marketing and there are exactly five B2B capabilities Plus still owns, plus two that need Advanced.

| Limitation on Basic | What it costs you in practice | Fixed by |
|---|---|---|
| Max 3 catalogs | You can only run three pricing tiers. A fourth customer segment has nowhere to go. | Plus (unlimited) |
| No direct company catalog | **No customer-specific negotiated pricing.** This is the big one. | Plus |
| No deposit requirements | Cannot take 30% upfront on a large or made-to-order job | Plus |
| No partial payments | Cannot record instalments against a single invoice | Plus |
| No payment per fulfilment | Cannot bill each shipment separately on backordered POs | Plus |
| No contextual checkout | Same checkout config for wholesale and retail | Advanced or Plus |
| No contextual storefront | Cannot show different homepage content to B2B buyers | Advanced or Plus |

Beyond the B2B module itself, Plus adds things that matter once you are running a serious wholesale operation: checkout extensibility (Checkout Branding API, Checkout Blocks), Shopify Functions, unlimited staff accounts, 9 expansion stores and unlimited staging stores, extended payment authorisation up to 30 days, 200 locations, and a 99.99% uptime SLA. [Full Plus feature list](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/plans-features/shopify-plus-plan). Pricing starts at $2,500/month on a one-year term.

## 5. How apps fill the gap (and what it costs you)

Here is the thing most app comparison articles never explain: **wholesale apps do not use Shopify's catalog system at all.**

They take a completely different route — **customer tags**.

The app writes a tag onto the customer record (`tier-gold`, `rahman-traders`, `distributor-north`), and tags have no limit. You can have five hundred. The app then matches the tag to a price rule and changes what the buyer sees.

It changes the price in one of three ways:

1. **Storefront JavaScript** — rewrites the displayed price after the buyer logs in
2. **Shopify Functions or automatic discounts** — deducts the difference at checkout
3. **Draft orders** — the app builds the order itself with custom line prices

![Native catalogs vs app-based pricing](images/03-native-vs-app-pricing.svg)

### The honest downsides

This approach genuinely solves the tier limit. But you should go in knowing the trade-offs:

- **Discount-vs-price display.** Many apps show $50 struck through with a $20 discount, rather than simply showing $30. Some B2B buyers dislike this, and it can look messy on invoices and in accounting exports.
- **Theme fragility.** JavaScript price rewriting can break when you update or switch themes. Test on a development store first.
- **Tax and VAT behaviour.** Discount-based pricing can compute tax differently from true price-list pricing. Verify this if you are in a VAT jurisdiction.
- **Conflicts with native B2B.** Running native catalogs *and* a pricing app simultaneously will produce inconsistent prices. Choose one.
- **Multi-currency friction.** Tag-based rules and Shopify Markets currency conversion do not always interact cleanly.

### What no app can replicate

Be realistic about this. **Partial payments, payment per fulfilment, and true checkout customisation are structural Plus features.** Apps can approximate deposits (see Downpay in section 7), but nothing bolts genuine split-payment reconciliation onto a Basic plan. If those are core to how you get paid, budget for Plus rather than fighting it.

## 6. Apps you genuinely need

Before you install anything, turn on the free things. A surprising number of merchants pay for apps that duplicate these.

### Turn these on first — all free

| Tool | What it covers | Link |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer accounts** | The foundation. Nothing in B2B works without it. | [Docs](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/customer-login-and-accounts) |
| **Shopify Forms** | Wholesale application form with approval gate | [Docs](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/companies-and-customers/company-account-requests) |
| **Trade theme** | Hides prices when logged out, quick order list built in | [Theme](https://themes.shopify.com/themes/trade) |
| **Shopify Flow** | Order tagging, payment reminders, new-company alerts | [Docs](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow) |

Now the paid ones that earn their keep.

### 6.1 A B2B pricing engine — *only if three tiers is not enough*

**Why:** the three-catalog cap and the inability to price per customer. This is the single most common reason to buy an app.

| App | Best for | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|
| [SparkLayer B2B & Wholesale](https://apps.shopify.com/sparklayer) | Most complete. Unlimited price lists, sales rep portal, quoting engine, ERP/Xero/QuickBooks sync | Free tier; ~$49–299/mo |
| [BSS: B2B & Wholesale Solution](https://apps.shopify.com/b2b-solution) | Widest feature set at the lowest price — custom pricing, registration forms, tax exemption | Free install; ~$25–100/mo |
| [Wholesale Gorilla](https://apps.shopify.com/wholesale-gorilla) | Bolting wholesale onto an existing DTC store with minimal fuss | ~$50+/mo |
| [Wholesale Pricing Discount B2B](https://apps.shopify.com/wholesale-pricing-discount) | Small catalogs, simple tiered pricing | ~$20–60/mo |

### 6.2 Invoicing

**Why:** a wholesale buyer has an accounts department. They need a proper PDF with the company name, tax registration number, PO reference and payment due date. Shopify's default order confirmation email is not an invoice.

- [Sufio: Professional Invoices](https://apps.shopify.com/sufio) — automatic, tax-compliant, strong multi-currency and VAT handling
- [Order Printer Pro](https://apps.shopify.com/order-printer-pro) — invoices, quotes and packing slips; the budget option

### 6.3 Conditional shipping rules

**Why:** wholesale freight is not flat-rate. "Free delivery over $500, but only for approved dealers" or "weight-banded rates outside the metro area" cannot be built in Shopify's native shipping settings.

- [Intuitive Shipping](https://apps.shopify.com/intuitive-shipping) — conditions on customer tag, weight, order value, zone
- [Advanced Shipping Rules](https://apps.shopify.com/advanced-shipping-rules) — similar, cheaper

### 6.4 Accounting sync

**Why:** selling on Net 30 means selling on credit. Who owes what, and who is overdue, becomes unmanageable by hand within a few months. In wholesale, cash flow is the business.

Xero or QuickBooks connectors sync orders and invoices automatically. If you use local accounting software, plan a CSV export or API integration early rather than as an afterthought.

### 6.5 Account approval — *if the native form is not enough*

**Why:** you never want a random visitor seeing wholesale prices. Shopify Forms handles the basic case. If you need trade licence uploads, VAT validation or a multi-step application, you need an app.

- [B2B Portal: Account & Approval](https://apps.shopify.com/b2b-account-registration-app) — multi-step registration, application review, order approvals
- [Addify – Approve New Customers](https://apps.shopify.com/approve-new-customers) — 14 custom field types, manual approval
- [B2B Account Approval EU](https://apps.shopify.com/b2b-portal-1) — VAT ID verification for European selling

## 7. Apps that are nice to have

These are not day-one purchases. Add them when the pain is real.

**Bulk and CSV ordering.** When a dealer orders 120 SKUs at once, clicking through product pages is impossible. The Trade theme has a quick order list, but no CSV upload. → [Upro B2B & Wholesale](https://apps.shopify.com/upro-b2b-and-wholesale-upload-csv-order)

**Credit limits and customer ledger.** "Block new orders if this account owes more than $5,000" does not exist natively. Available in Upro and SparkLayer's higher tiers. At small scale, a Shopify Flow alert is a workable substitute.

**Deposits and partial payments.** Plus-only natively. The closest workaround is [Downpay](https://apps.shopify.com/downpay).

**Tax exemption and VAT validation.** Resellers are often tax-exempt. Native B2B handles this at the company-location level; automated validation needs an app. BSS has it built in; at enterprise scale, Avalara AvaTax.

**Content gating.** Restricting specific collections or pages to approved dealers → [Locksmith](https://apps.shopify.com/locksmith)

**ERP connector.** If inventory lives outside Shopify, you will oversell without a sync. Common options: Cin7, Brightpearl, Unleashed, Linnworks, NetSuite.

**Search and filtering.** Wholesale buyers do not browse — they search by SKU. Past ~500 products, consider Boost AI Search or Searchanise.

**Reporting.** Which dealer buys what, and which SKUs move in which channel. Basic's reporting is thin. Report Pundit or Better Reports fill it.

## 8. Request a Quote: does Shopify do this natively?

This deserves its own section because the answer is genuinely nuanced, and a lot of merchants get it wrong in both directions.

### What Shopify does give you

**Draft orders are Shopify's built-in quoting mechanism.** As the merchant you can:

- Set a [custom price on a line item](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/create-orders/create-draft#set-custom-line-item-prices) — your negotiated number
- [Lock the price](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/checkout-and-orders/draft-orders#lock-unlock-prices) so the quote holds even if the product price changes later
- Reserve inventory against the pending quote
- Send an invoice or checkout link with payment terms attached
- Use **checkout-to-draft**, where the buyer builds a cart and hits "Submit for approval", landing it in your Drafts queue with prices locked by default

On paper, that is a quoting workflow. Which is why some people conclude no app is needed.

### What it does not give you

![Native draft orders vs an RFQ app](images/05-quote-flow-native-vs-rfq.svg)

| Capability | Native Shopify |
|---|---|
| Buyer-initiated "Request a Quote" button on a product page | ✘ |
| Counter-offers / negotiation thread | ✘ |
| Quote expiry ("valid for 7 days") | ✘ |
| Quote versioning and revision history | ✘ |
| A "my quotes" area in the customer account | ✘ |
| Quoting a guest or lead without first creating a Company | ✘ |
| Hide price and replace with a quote button | ✘ (Trade theme hides prices, but adds no button) |
| Quote-to-order conversion analytics | ✘ |
| Merchant-built quote with custom pricing | ✔ |
| Price lock on a sent quote | ✔ |
| Buyer submits cart for approval | ✔ (checkout-to-draft, per company location) |

The critical distinction: **native quoting is always merchant-initiated.** Checkout-to-draft comes closest, but it is a setting on the company location — not a per-order choice the buyer makes — and it means "submit this order for approval", not "tell me what this would cost".

### So who actually needs an RFQ app?

- Custom manufacturing and made-to-order businesses
- Project-based selling where every deal is priced individually
- Industries where published pricing is commercially sensitive
- Anyone who wants to capture quote requests from **leads who are not yet set up as companies**

If your pricing is tiered and predictable, native draft orders will carry you. If every deal is negotiated, an RFQ app is a must-have, not a nice-to-have.

> **If you are building one:** hand off to a native draft order on approval. Keeping quotes entirely inside your own database means invoicing, payment terms, tax calculation and reporting all fall outside Shopify's pipeline — and merchants will feel that friction immediately.

## 10. How to test your B2B setup before going live

Never configure B2B directly on a live store. Test it properly first.

### Choosing a test environment

| Option | What you get | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Partner development store** (free) | Full B2B feature set for building and testing | Create from the Partner Dashboard; real transactions are not possible |
| **Plus staging store** | Exact production parity, including Plus-only features | Unlimited on Plus |
| **[Shopify Plus trial](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/plus-trial)** | Trial Plus-only features | 1–2 months depending on store status |
| **Paid Basic store + Bogus Gateway** | Real Basic-plan constraints, no money moves | Best for validating the app-gap decision |

### The official procedure

Per [Shopify's testing guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/getting-started/testing):

1. Activate **customer accounts** — B2B login depends on it
2. Go to **Customers → Companies** and create a test company with at least one location
3. Configure the location: catalog, payment terms, checkout settings, tax exemption
4. Create a **test customer profile** inside that company — use an `@example.com` email, which Shopify treats as safe and which will not trip spam filters
5. Activate the [Bogus Gateway](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/test-orders/payments-test-mode#bogus-gateway) for simulated payments — card number `1` succeeds, `2` fails, `3` throws an exception; any future expiry and any CVV
6. Log in to the storefront as that buyer
7. Place a [test order](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/test-orders)
8. If using B2B with Markets, [preview the storefront per market](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/markets/managing#review)

### Pre-launch QA checklist

- [ ] A logged-out visitor cannot see wholesale prices
- [ ] Catalog price displays correctly for the assigned company location
- [ ] Volume price breaks trigger at the right thresholds
- [ ] Quantity rules block invalid quantities in the cart **and** at checkout
- [ ] Payment terms appear at checkout and the order shows the correct due date
- [ ] Checkout-to-draft routes the order to Drafts, not Orders
- [ ] PO number field appears and persists onto the order
- [ ] A second contact on the same company sees the same catalog
- [ ] An "ordering only" contact cannot edit company details
- [ ] Reorder rebuilds the cart at correct B2B pricing
- [ ] Tax exemption applies on an exempt location
- [ ] Flow automations fire as expected
- [ ] *Plus:* deposit collected at checkout, balance invoiced later
- [ ] *Plus:* a direct company catalog overrides the market catalog

## 11. Seven mistakes new wholesale merchants make

**1. Buying apps before exhausting the free tools.** Native B2B, the Trade theme, Shopify Forms and Flow cover a genuine first 20–30 accounts at almost no cost.

**2. Running two pricing systems at once.** Native catalogs plus a pricing app will produce conflicting prices. One per store.

**3. Not adding up app costs.** Six apps at $30/month is $2,160 a year. Run the maths against a plan upgrade before committing.

**4. Ignoring theme dependency.** Apps that rewrite prices in JavaScript break on theme updates. Test before you launch and after every theme change.

**5. Not checking how prices display at checkout.** Some apps show a discount rather than the true price. Verify this before buying — it affects invoices and buyer perception.

**6. Skipping test orders.** A free development store costs nothing. Configuration errors found in production cost real money and real customer trust.

**7. Forgetting the Markets prerequisite.** On Basic, Grow and Advanced, catalogs will not work until the store is on new Shopify Markets. People lose days to this.

## 12. A decision framework you can actually use

Answer three questions honestly.

**How many price tiers do you need?**
Three or fewer → native B2B is enough, skip the pricing app entirely. More, or per-customer negotiated pricing → you need SparkLayer or BSS.

**Do you take money upfront?**
Deposits and instalments are structurally Plus. Downpay approximates deposits; nothing properly replaces partial payments.

**Do you have sales reps?**
If someone places orders on a buyer's behalf, or takes orders at trade shows, native Shopify is weak here. That is app or Plus territory.

---

## The short version

Start with native B2B. Three catalogs, the Trade theme, Shopify Forms and Flow will carry your first twenty to thirty wholesale accounts at close to zero cost.

Then, when you feel a specific pain — *"I need a fourth price tier"*, *"I've lost track of who owes me money"*, *"my rep is taking orders on the phone and typing them in himself"* — buy the app that solves **that** pain.

Apps should be the answer to a problem you already have, not a hedge against one you might.
